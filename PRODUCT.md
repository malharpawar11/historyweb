# Product

## What it is
A single-page research site for a high school Real History project by Malhar Pawar. It answers one question: *What was the effect of the public arena-funding dispute on the SuperSonics' departure from Seattle, 2006–2008?*

## Who uses it and where
- **A teacher and classmates**, in a live 6-minute presentation on a school laptop and projector, followed by questions.
- **The teacher again, later**, reading it on a laptop to grade it against the rubric (research question, themes, two thinking skills, narrative, format, sample).
- Visitors arrive from a link. There is no sign-in, no backend, no checkout.

## Mode
Persuade and Read together. The page argues a position, so it earns attention with real motion and 3D. The argument is also read closely, so text must stay plain and easy to read.

## What it must do
1. State the question and answer it in plain words.
2. Show the evidence for each cause, whose voice it carries, and what limits it (Skill 6C).
3. Show two processes side by side and where one pushed the other (Skill 5B).
4. Let the audience weigh the causes themselves and compare.
5. Say what changed by 2026, because the Sonics might return.

## Constraints
- Runs smoothly on a school laptop and a projector. No jank.
- Still makes full sense if 3D fails, WebGL is missing, or reduced motion is on.
- Every claim names its source. No invented numbers or quotes.
- No framework. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Deployable as static files on Vercel.

## Voice
Short, specific, plain sentences. Say what the thing is and what the evidence shows. No marketing filler, no em dashes, no arrows glued to links.

## References and feel
Apple product pages, Lusion and Resn for real 3D and scroll-driven motion. Serious and readable, with the 3D and motion as the centerpiece. The Sonics green and gold stays as the accent.
